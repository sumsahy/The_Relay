Question 2: What if 100 users encounter the error?
Good intuition
You mentioned offsetting their waiting times. This is an important idea in distributed systems called jitter.

Imagine all 100 requests receive a 429 error and are told to retry after exactly five seconds.

Without jitter

All requests retry simultaneously.

A second burst of requests after five seconds.

With jitter

Requests retry at slightly different times.

Retry attempts distributed over a time window.

A common implementation uses exponential backoff with jitter.

The waiting period increases after each failure, while a random component prevents requests from synchronising.

However, there is another important consideration: jitter alone doesn't solve the problem.

We also need to control the total number of outgoing requests. Otherwise, 100 requests can still overwhelm the provider, just at slightly different times.

This is where rate limiters and queues become important.
